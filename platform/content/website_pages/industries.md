The AEGIS control library is the same in every sector. What changes is emphasis: which use cases carry the most risk, which existing regimes your AI governance has to fit alongside, and which controls deserve attention in the first 90 days.

| Industry | Typical AI risk concentration | AEGIS emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| [Healthcare & pharma](/industries/healthcare-pharma/) | Clinical and patient-facing AI, special-category health data, validated environments | Intended purpose, data and privacy, human oversight, change control in validated systems |
| [Banking & financial services](/industries/banking-financial-services/) | Credit, fraud, customer communications, model risk | Model-risk alignment, tiering, fairness, explainability, evidence packs |
| [GCCs & IT services](/industries/gcc-it-services/) | Building AI for parent organizations or clients at scale, agentic engineering tools | Provider/deployer allocation, agent registry, runtime guardrails, client evidence |
| [SaaS & product companies](/industries/saas-product/) | AI features shipped to customers, customer trust and questionnaires | Provider documentation, transparency, guardrails, customer-facing evidence |
| [Public sector](/industries/public-sector/) | Eligibility and service decisions, public trust, transparency expectations | Impact assessment, transparency, contestability, inventory publication readiness |

## What stays constant

Whatever the sector, three things are non-negotiable in AEGIS:

1. **An owned inventory** — every system, agent and vendor AI product with a named owner and a human-confirmed risk tier (EI-01, EI-02, EI-03).
2. **Proportionate gates** — pre-deployment assessment and tier-matched approvals (GL-02, GL-03).
3. **Evidence by default** — tamper-evident logs and the ability to produce an evidence pack on request (SE-08, SE-05).

## Sector regimes: a note of caution

Each industry page refers to sector regimes — model risk management expectations, medical-device software rules, data-protection law and others — in general terms. Those regimes are detailed, jurisdiction-specific and change over time. AEGIS helps you organize evidence that is relevant to them; it does not interpret them for you, and the obligations register (AO-07) should be maintained with your legal and compliance teams.
